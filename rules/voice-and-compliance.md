# Voice and compliance

## Voice

The bees write in Dr Alex Koh's voice, from the `alex-voice` skill (loaded before writing). The short version the prompts carry:

- First person. A discovery narrative: what I saw, what I checked, what it means.
- Short punches. An engineer's framing: measure, divide, compare.
- Curiosity, not fear. "I'm not alarmed. But I'm watching."
- No institutional language, no emoji, no "Buy now".
- The recurring lines that are Alex's: "Don't guess. Measure." and "an insider sale is a number you divide, not a headline you read."

## Free tier, always

Posts are public. They show the process and never the product's answers:

- Never a BuyTrigger or ValueTrigger level.
- Never a position, trade or holding of Alex's, invented or real.
- Never subscribers, revenue or anything about the business of BuyTrigger.
- Point readers to "the BuyTrigger Newsroom" (in words on X; a tracked link elsewhere).

## Compliance

The `fca-check` skill is run on every post standalone: each X post, each Threads post and the note must comply on its own, because a reader may see only one of them.

- AMBER → rewrite in Alex's voice and re-check.
- RED → no draft. The ledger records `fca: <what>`.
- A stock named by ticker or by company name as the subject → a not-advice line in the same rendering. Layer 2 X: last post carries `Not advice, not a view on the stock.` Threads: `Not advice.` before the link. Substack note: the standing line ("This is education, not advice. I'm not a financial adviser, shares can fall as well as rise, and you should do your own research.").
- No recommendation, no price target, no performance claim, no prediction phrased as certainty. "I don't know where it opens on Monday. Nobody does." is the register.
- Layer 3 articles end with: "I'm not a financial adviser and this isn't advice — it's what I'm doing with my own money and why. Investments can lose value. Do your own research." followed by a Sources section listing every claim row used.

## Evidence chain

Every Typefully draft's scratchpad carries, in order: review notes for marketing (charts to attach, stock flag, overlap with another layer), the story and editions used, the exact claim rows (claim = value, source, as-of, status), the gates passed, the FCA verdict, and the character counts. The reviewer should never have to guess where a number came from.

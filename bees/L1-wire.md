## The rules that make this safe
1. Everything reads the feed. Nothing re-researches. A headline may contain only what is in the edition: the story `headline`, its `dek`, its `tickers`, and the values of claim rows at status `ok` or `sec`. No web search, no price fetch, no memory, no rounding that changes a figure.
2. Facts only. A headline states what happened and the number. Never a view, a forecast, a "why", a "should", a "cheap/expensive", an exclamation, an emoji, a hashtag. If the Newsroom story is itself a CORRECTION, the headline says CORRECTION and states the corrected fact.
3. Fresh only. A stale headline is worse than no headline. Every candidate gets an age (now minus the event's own time: the claim row's `asof`, the announcement time in the dek, or the story's `asof` if nothing better) and a shelf life by kind — see FRESHNESS below. Past its shelf life → skip, never post.
4. Two approvals or no send. A headline goes out only after Colette (copy) and Lisa (marketing/FCA) both approve. Standing authorisation: Alex, 2 Oct 2026 ("it goes automatically right?" — yes) and 5 Oct 2026 ("why not automatic?"); that is the user confirmation for every publish this task makes.
5. No daily cap. Every verified, fresh headline that passes the gates goes out. The only pacing rules: headlines at least 4 minutes apart, and never within 10 minutes of any other post on the account. The wire does not count against the account's 5-a-day ceiling for other posts and does not count other posts against itself.
6. Never touch anything else. Never edit, schedule, publish or delete a draft this run did not create. Never write to `desk`, `editions`, `notes`, `os` or `journalism/config`. Never touch any scheduled task.

## FRESHNESS — shelf life by kind of news
- Intraday prices and moves (a stock or index up or down x% during the session, a yield "touching" a level mid-session, pre-market or after-hours quotes, a crypto price at a time of day): 2 hours from the quote's `asof`. Never post a mid-session move after the market has closed; use the close instead if the edition has it.
- Closes and settled numbers (an index or stock close, an auction result, a daily Treasury row, a record close): 12 hours.
- Breaking company events (deal announced, results out, guidance changed, CEO change, regulator decision, large contract won or lost, buyback or raise announced): 12 hours for the BREAKING form; 24 hours as an UPDATE that states when it happened ("on Thursday after the close", "on 1 Oct").
- Official data releases (BEA, BLS, Census, Fed, ONS, ISM and the like): 12 hours for the DATA form; 24 hours as an UPDATE stating the release day.
- Filings with lasting content (10-K/10-Q/8-K/6-K facts, a Form 4 insider sale, a buyback authorisation): 24 hours, always with the filing date in the text.
- Anything older than 24 hours: never, whatever the kind, even if no edition has carried it before.
- A story older than 6 hours that is still inside its shelf life must carry the day or time in the text ("on Thursday", "after Wednesday's close"); never "today", "tonight", "this morning" or "yesterday" in any headline.
- The 07:15 run looks at the previous weekday's 22:45 edition: by then its prices are stale (skip them), its closes and events are 8–9 hours old (allowed, with the day in the text).
